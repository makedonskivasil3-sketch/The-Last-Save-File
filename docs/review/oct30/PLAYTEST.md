# Retest the integrated build

Record build/commit, device, OS, browser or native executable, input method,
text size, and reduced-effects settings. Use a separate test profile so the
player's own progress remains intact. Record screen/input with consent.

## First: observe without explaining

Ask the player to play until they have found something useful and used it to
change the world. Let them choose routes. Note:

- What do they think Rue wants now? Why?
- Can they predict what lies beyond an exit before crossing it?
- Do they enter a map accidentally or lose their direction on arrival?
- Which repeated journeys feel empty?
- Can they identify the wick, lamp and hatch before touching them?
- Do they understand that the repaired route is a shortcut, not a new ending?
- Does Wren's later response make the action feel noticed?

If they never reach the workshop, record the route/time before offering the
single hint: "There is a south stair in the Archive." Do not count a hinted
completion as unaided discovery.

## Then: deliberate recovery checks

- Save and exit before taking the wick; relaunch.
- Save and exit while carrying it; relaunch.
- Save and exit after the repair; relaunch.
- Use the hatch in both directions. Check journal Objects, Leads and Places.
- On an actual phone, rotate mid-movement and mid-threat, switch apps, return,
  and resume. The game must not advance behind the portrait pause. The
  festival/cold-open interruption paths also need physical-device testing.
- Try one threshold with touch, pointer and keyboard where supported. Describe
  the exact snag or unexpected response instead of just rating "movement".

## Questions after play

1. Where did you feel you were making progress? Where did you feel sent back?
2. What looked unfinished, confusing or inconsistent? Point to a moment.
3. Who did you care about, and what did you do that made you care?
4. Did the save behave as you expected?
5. Would you voluntarily continue? What would stop you?

Use observations to choose the next mission/art milestone. A few friends are
valuable early feedback, not proof of sales or a statistically reliable market
estimate. Physical Windows/controller/iPhone and human listening remain real
checks, not things automation can stand in for.
